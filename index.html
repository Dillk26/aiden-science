Part 1: The Chemistry of Water

Questions 1 – 3 • Polarity, Hydrogen Bonding, and Properties Supporting Life

Question 1

1. Label the atoms that make up a water molecule. Label the charges that accompany the atoms with a + or −.

Water Molecule (H₂O)

Atoms: 1 Oxygen atom covalently bonded to 2 Hydrogen atoms (H₂O).

Charges: Oxygen pulls shared electrons strongly, giving it a partial negative charge (− or δ−). The two Hydrogens are left with partial positive charges (+ or δ+).

Question 2

2. Why is water important for living organisms to survive?

Focus on cellular functions and biological homeostasis.

Water is the universal medium for life because:

  • Universal Solvent: Dissolves minerals, nutrients, and waste for transport through blood and cytoplasm.
  • Medium for Chemical Reactions: Metabolic processes occur dissolved in cellular water.
  • Temperature Regulation: High heat capacity prevents drastic temperature swings in cells.
  • Evaporative Cooling: Sweating removes excess heat to maintain homeostasis.
Question 3

3. Define these properties of water:

a. Polarity
b. Cohesion
c. Adhesion
d. Surface Tension
e. Capillarity
a. Polarity Unequal sharing of electrons resulting in a slightly negative oxygen pole and slightly positive hydrogen poles.
b. Cohesion The attraction between molecules of the same substance (water sticking to water via hydrogen bonds).
c. Adhesion The attraction between molecules of different substances (water sticking to glass or plant xylem walls).
d. Surface Tension The elastic tendency of a liquid's surface caused by cohesive forces pulling surface molecules inward, allowing small insects to walk on water.
e. Capillarity (Capillary Action) The upward movement of water through narrow tubes against gravity, resulting from adhesion to tube walls combined with cohesion between water molecules.

Part 2: Food, Digestion & Carbon Backbone

Questions 4 – 7 • Nutrient Flow, Monomers vs. Polymers, and Carbon Bonding

Question 4

4. What purpose does food serve for organisms?

Identify the two primary physiological purposes.

1. Source of Energy: Broken down during cellular respiration to produce ATP to fuel cellular processes.

2. Source of Raw Materials (Matter): Provides essential building blocks to grow new cells, repair tissues, and synthesize macromolecules.

Question 5 • Updated Pathway

5. What is the flow of food through the human digestive system?

The exact anatomical sequence through the alimentary canal.

Official 9th Grade Flow of Food:

Mouth → Esophagus → Stomach → Small Intestine → 6. Large Intestine (Colon) → 7. Rectum → 8. Anus

Note: Food passes sequentially through this tube. Solid wastes are compacted in the colon, stored in the rectum, and eliminated through the anus.

Question 6

6. Why is carbon the basis of life?

Relate to carbon's atomic valence structure.

Carbon has 4 valence electrons, enabling it to form 4 strong covalent bonds simultaneously.

It can bond with itself and other elements to build an infinite variety of straight chains, branched chains, and rings.

Question 7

7. Label a monomer and the polymer. Define both terms.

Monomer: A small, single molecular building block subunit (e.g., glucose, amino acid).

Polymer: A large macromolecule composed of many repeating monomers joined by covalent bonds (e.g., starch, polypeptide chain).

Question 22

Name key biological functions performed by proteins.

• Enzymes: Speed up biochemical reactions.

• Structure: Collagen in skin, keratin in hair and nails.

• Transport: Hemoglobin moving oxygen in red blood cells.

• Immunity: Antibodies defending against pathogens.

• Movement: Actin and myosin in muscle fibers.

Part 4: Enzymes & Chemical Reactions

Questions 23 – 32 • Catalysts, Activation Energy, Lock-and-Key Model, and Denaturation

23. Activation Energy

The minimum amount of energy needed to start a chemical reaction.

24. Catalyst Role

Speeds up reactions by lowering activation energy without being consumed.

25. What is an Enzyme?

A biological catalyst made of folded protein.

26. Denaturing Factors

Extreme temperature and drastic changes in pH.

27. Specific Substrate? ✅ TRUE

Active site matches one specific substrate.

28. Many Different Reactions? ❌ FALSE

Enzymes typically catalyze only one reaction.

29. End in "-ase"? ✅ TRUE

e.g., Lactase, Amylase, Lipase.

30. Macromolecule Class? 🥩 PROTEINS

Formed from folded amino acid chains.

Question 31

31. Most enzymes...

Correct: d. are sensitive to changes in temperature or pH

Enzymes are not consumed (a is false), they lower activation energy (b is false), and they strain/weaken substrate bonds (c is false).
